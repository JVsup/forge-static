Bot Census

Are you tired of going in to a raid blind? Do you want to know who Nikita is sending to prevent you from extracting? Well welcome Bot Census!

Description

Overview

An in-raid overlay that tallies every AI on the map with you, custom factions included. Every line carries its own icon — a scav in his balaclava, the BTR on its eight wheels, the cultists’ sigil, the Goons as a trio — so the panel reads at a glance instead of as a wall of words. It re-scans on a timer, so a patrol that spawns deep into a raid turns up on the next tick rather than never, and it eases in with the rest of the HUD on load instead of snapping over it.

Nothing to configure to get going. Drop it in and launch.

What it counts

  • Custom factions — RUAF, Remnant, UNTAR and Black Division, each on its own line. They register above the vanilla spawn-type ceiling of 67. Bot Census pulls names from MoreBotsAPI’s live registry by reflection rather than hardcoding a list, so factions added after this was written show up too. With MoreBotsAPI absent it falls back to the known ID ranges and carries on.
  • The Goons on one line, so you know the trio is on the map before you walk into them.
  • The BTR gunner, separately.
  • AI PMCs, on their own line, kept off the scav side.
  • Raiders and Rogues on their own rows, and bosses apart from their guards, so you can tell whether the boss himself is still standing or you’ve only got his escort left. Both pairs merge back into single rows from F12 if you want a shorter panel.
  • Scavs, bosses, cultists and the infected event, with the vanilla sorting oddities put right — crazyAssaultEvent reads as a Scav rather than a boss, and cursedAssault, assaultGroup and the misspelled sectactPriestEvent all land where they belong. Anything genuinely unknown drops into Other instead of vanishing.
  • Everything, added up — a Total Bots line under a rule at the foot of the panel. It counts off the tally rather than the rows above it, so anything you’ve merged or hidden still adds up.

Modded bosses

A modded faction that fields a leader gets him on his own line with his escort beneath — a boss and three guards read as 1 and 3 rather than a single 4, so you can tell whether the boss himself is still up or you’re mopping up guards. It works off the faction’s own registry rather than a hardcoded list, so a faction added after this was written splits the same way, while flat squads with nobody in charge keep their single line. Merge them back from F12 with the same toggle the vanilla bosses use.

Where a faction has a crest of its own it gets one — Black Division takes the scorpion, and its leader and guards carry the same crest under a crown and over a sword.

Installation

Requirements

Nothing. It runs on its own.

Two optional integrations, each detected at runtime and ignored if absent:

Mod GUID What you lose without it
MoreBotsAPI com.morebotsapi.tacticaltoaster Live faction names. Custom factions still get counted off the known ID ranges, they just won’t be named from the registry.
Fika com.fika.core Co-op.

Neither is a hard dependency, so Bot Census loads either way.

Steps

  1. Download the archive.
  2. Extract it into your SPT folder — the one holding EscapeFromTarkov.exe and BepInEx.
  3. Merge when Windows asks. BotCensus lands in BepInEx/plugins.
  4. Launch. The panel appears top-right once you’re in a raid.

Client-side only. Nothing goes in user/mods.

Configuration

In-game F12, or edit BepInEx/config/com.sipto.botcensus.cfg.

Setting Default Notes
Enable on Master toggle
Only In Raid on Keeps it off the menu and hideout
Font Size 16 10–30
Offset Right / Top 20 / 40 Panel position
Use Tarkov Font on The game’s Bender face, with fallbacks
Show Icons on Off drops the icon column and narrows the panel
Background Opacity 0.72 0 clear to 1 solid
Split Rogue And Raider on Off merges them into one row
Split Boss And Guard on Off merges them into one row, modded bosses included
Update Interval 5s 5s / 10s / 15s / 30s / 1min

Per-row visibility

Every bot type also has its own visibility mode:

  • Always — keeps the row up even at zero
  • WhenPresent — only shows it while some are alive
  • Hidden — drops it entirely

PMC, Scav and Total default to Always, everything else to WhenPresent.

Compatibility

Fika

On a Fika raid it counts off the shared player list, so your numbers agree with the host’s. It only reads state and draws to your own screen — no patches, nothing networked. Only you need it installed.

Panel position

It draws in the top-right. If you’ve already got a counter living there, run one or the other, or push Offset Top down until they clear each other.

Bot Census 1.4.0

One addition: a bindable key that shows and hides the panel.

  • F12 → 1. General → Toggle Key. Unbound by default — click it and press whatever you like.
  • It flips the same switch as the Enable checkbox, so the two always agree, and the panel still eases in the way it always has.
  • It works while other keys are held, so toggling on the move is fine.

Nothing else has changed.

The “it stopped counting” fix. A few of you reported the panel going blank after a raid or two — still drawing, insisting the map was empty, until an off/on in F12 woke it up.

Found it: the hideout runs a game world of its own, and once the panel latched onto that one it stayed latched — a world with no bots in it, faithfully reported as such every raid after.

The panel now reads whichever world the game itself registers as current and treats the hideout as not-a-raid, so there’s nothing left to latch onto. The F12 ritual is retired. Thanks to Mozzer, routaHHH and JobTown for reports precise enough to pin the pattern down.

The Fika fix. If you join a friend’s raid rather than host it, every custom boss and faction used to tally as “Custom” — the registry the panel names factions from only ever fills on the machine that owns the bots. The panel now reads what each faction mod registers on every machine instead, so Wedge, Black Division and the rest label correctly on a client too.

One wrinkle that fix surfaced: a mod’s menu section isn’t a faction name. Blackout files its guards under “Black Division”, which would have folded them into the real Black Division row — so the known factions stay pinned ahead of that lookup, and Blackout’s crew gets its own row.

Hosts and solo players were never affected; this is worth updating for before your next co-op session.
